# Credits

None of the frameworks in here are mine. What's mine is which ones I
reach for, in what order, the opinions in each skill, and how they hand
off to each other. Here's where the underlying ideas come from.

## Frameworks the skills lean on
| Idea | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Opportunity Solution Tree | Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* |
| Jobs to be done | Clayton Christensen; the switch interview from Bob Moesta |
| Build, measure, learn; MVP | Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* |
| Riskiest Assumption Test | Rik Higham's critique of the MVP, widely adapted since |
| RICE scoring | Intercom's product team |
| ICE scoring | Sean Ellis |
| Pirate metrics (AARRR) | Dave McClure |
| Value Proposition Canvas | Strategyzer (Alex Osterwalder and team) |
| Story mapping | Jeff Patton |
| Design sprint methods | Jake Knapp; Google's Design Sprint Kit |
| Usability heuristics | Nielsen Norman Group |
| Product sense case structure (used in my interview prep, not in this repo) | Ben Erez, via Lenny's Newsletter |

## Method guides
The 14 guides in `frameworks/method-guides/` are my own write-ups of
widely used workshop and research methods. Crazy 8s comes from Google's
Design Sprint Kit, Story Mapping from Jeff Patton, and the Value
Proposition Exercise builds on Strategyzer's canvas. The rest link to
the sources I learned them from, mostly Nielsen Norman Group and Mind
the Product.

## Humaniser
Built from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" page, plus patterns I kept
spotting in LinkedIn posts and in Claude's own replies.

## Structure
The idea of a "PM operating system" with a navigable hub was inspired by
other PM OS products I'd seen. The skills, context layer, playbooks and
example are my own.

If I've missed a credit, open an issue and I'll fix it.
