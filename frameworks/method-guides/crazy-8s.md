# Crazy 8s

*Ideas · 45 min · Ideation · 4 to 8 people, ideally not all PMs*

## Purpose

Eight ideas in eight minutes, sketched rather than written. It's the fastest way I know to get a room past its first, most obvious answer, and to get engineers and ops sketching instead of watching the PM present.

## When I reach for it

- The problem's agreed but everyone's quietly attached to the same solution
- Straight after a How Might We, when there's a sharp question to answer

## How I run it

1. **Frame the question (5 min).** One How Might We on the wall. If people can't agree on the question, stop here. Crazy 8s on a fuzzy problem gives you eight fuzzy ideas.
2. **Warm up (2 min).** Everyone sketches one deliberately terrible idea. It takes the pressure off drawing.
3. **Fold and sketch (8 min).** A4 folded into eight boxes, one minute per box, timer visible. Sketches, not sentences.
4. **Silent gallery (5 min).** Pin everything up. People read in silence and dot-vote on the boxes that interest them.
5. **Steal and remix (8 min).** Round two: each person takes one idea that isn't theirs and pushes it further.
6. **Pick (10 min).** Vote on the top three. The person who owns the problem makes the final call, not the loudest voice.
7. **Leave with tests, not features.** For each of the top three, write the riskiest assumption and the cheapest way to test it.

## Where it goes wrong

- The most senior person sketches the "right answer" in box one and everyone else draws variations of it. Have seniors go last in the gallery.
- Eight boxes of words, not sketches. Words get judged on how they're phrased, which isn't the point.
- It ends on a vote and nothing happens. Without step 7 this is a nice hour, not discovery.

## What you walk out with

Three ideas, each with its riskiest assumption and a test. Step 7 feeds straight into an Assumption Map.
