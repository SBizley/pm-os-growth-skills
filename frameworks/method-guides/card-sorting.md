# Card Sorting

*Evaluate · 2 hrs · Validation Solution*

# **Purpose**

Discover how users naturally group and label information - to ensure your navigation, menus, or settings match real mental models.

# **When to Use**

- When designing or restructuring information architecture (IA)
- Before building navigation or menu systems
- To validate naming, categorisation, or grouping logic

# **How to Run**

**Duration:** 1–2 hours

**Materials:** Physical index cards or digital tool (e.g. OptimalSort, Miro, UXtweak)

# **Steps:**

1. **List items** users will need to categorise (features, pages, products, etc.).
2. **Choose format:**
    - **Open sort** → users create their own categories (good for exploration).
    - **Closed sort** → users assign items to predefined categories (good for validation).
3. Recruit 15–30 users (remote or in-person).
4. Observe how they group and name categories.
5. Cluster similar patterns to reveal dominant structures.
6. Use findings to design or refine your site map, menu, or IA labels.

# **Example**

30 users sorted “the product app” features → 80 % grouped *“Transaction history”*, *“Export CSV”*, *“Disputes”* together under “Payments.”

Result → IA renamed “Payments & Reports” to match user vocabulary.

**Tips**

- Keep list size under ~50 items to avoid fatigue.
- Combine with **Tree Testing** afterwards to verify that structure works in navigation.
- Use visual clustering or dendrograms for analysis.
