# Quantify the Problem

*Understand · Ongoing · Understanding the problem*

# **Purpose**

Measure how big or costly a problem actually is - in frequency, impact, or business value - so you can prioritise effectively and make data-driven decisions.

# **When to Use**

- After defining a clear problem statement
- When stakeholders ask, “How big is this problem, really?”
- Before deciding whether to invest in solving it

# **How to Run**

**Duration:** 1–2 hours (analysis), variable for data gathering

**Materials:** Product analytics, support data, survey tools

# **Steps:**

- **Define measurable signals.**
    
    Translate your problem into metrics that reflect its magnitude.
    
    > e.g. “Merchants can’t trust payout times” → % of support tickets mentioning payouts or average refreshes per user per day.
    > 
- **Choose appropriate quantitative methods** (based on NN/g):
    - **Web or App Analytics:** Detect frequency and behavioural trends in live environments.
    - **Quantitative Usability Testing (Benchmarking):** Measure task success rates or time-on-task across versions.
    - **A/B or Multivariate Testing:** Compare different UI designs or messaging variants.
    - **Surveys or Questionnaires:** Quantify user sentiment (e.g. SUS, NPS, satisfaction scores).
    - **Card Sorting / Tree Testing:** Validate IA clarity and navigation success rates.
    - **Desirability or Eyetracking Studies:** Quantify aesthetic appeal and visual attention.
- **Collect enough data for significance.**
    
    Target ≥ 30–35 participants for usability benchmarking or >100 responses for surveys to ensure reliable signals.
    
- **Synthesise the numbers.**
    
    Combine *frequency* (how often it happens) × *impact* (how much it matters) to prioritise problems.
    
- **Visualise the evidence.**
    
    Create quick bar or scatter plots to show which problems dominate by volume or cost.
    

# **Example**

Quantitative usability study shows only **42 %** of merchants complete onboarding without support, and analytics confirm

**18 %** drop off at the verification step → high-impact, measurable bottleneck.

# **Tips**

- Use proxy metrics when direct ones aren’t available.
- Combine *occurrence rate* (frequency) with *business loss* (impact).
- Re-check data post-solution to confirm improvement.
