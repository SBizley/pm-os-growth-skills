# Five Whys

*Understand · 30 min · Discovery, Just starting, Problem-Solving*

## Purpose

Get to the root cause of a problem by repeatedly asking "why?" instead of stopping at surface-level explanations.

## When to Use

- After an unexpected failure or challenge
- When symptoms are obvious but the underlying cause isn't
- To avoid wasting energy fixing superficial issues

## How to Run

**Duration:** 30-60 minutes

**Materials needed:** Whiteboard or digital workspace, sticky notes

**Steps:**

1. Gather the people directly involved in or impacted by the problem.
2. Choose a facilitator to keep the discussion focused.
3. Write down the problem clearly.
4. Ask: "Why did this happen?" Write down the answer.
5. Take that answer and again ask: "Why did this happen?"
6. Repeat this process five times (or until you feel you've reached a root cause).
7. Capture potential solutions as they emerge.
8. Summarise agreed actions and assign ownership.

## Example

- Why did the robot stop? → A fuse blew.
- Why did the fuse blow? → Circuit overloaded.
- Why overloaded? → Bearings locked due to poor lubrication.
- Why poor lubrication? → Oil pump not circulating enough oil.
- Why pump failing? → Intake clogged with metal shavings.

**Root cause:** No filter on the pump.

## Tips

- Five is a rule of thumb - sometimes you'll get there in three, sometimes in seven
- Works best in a blame-free environment
- Document the chain of reasoning for future reference
