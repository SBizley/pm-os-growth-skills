# 🧭 Problem Statement - Advanced

## 🎯 Purpose

Move beyond surface-level problem framing to create **a structured, evidence-based understanding** of what problem we’re solving, why it matters, and what success looks like.

This framework ensures that **every initiative starts with a validated problem hypothesis** before teams invest time in solutioning or delivery.

At many companies, we’re not short on ideas - our challenge is connecting them to **real customer needs and measurable business outcomes**.

The advanced problem statement helps teams do exactly that.

## 🧩 When to Use

Use this framework when you need to:

- Frame or reframe an initiative before solution design or delivery.
- Align multiple stakeholders on **what the actual problem is**.
- Prioritise among competing opportunities.
- Reassess or pivot a current initiative after new insights emerge.

It’s most valuable during these **discovery funnel stages**:

> Opportunities → Context & Problem Definition → Problem Validation → Ideation & Solutionising.
> 

## ⚙️ How to Run

**Duration:** 60–90 minutes

**Materials:** Whiteboard, Miro/Notion template, customer insights, supporting data

### Step 1: Define the Observation

Start with the **observable customer behaviour or outcome** that indicates a problem.

> “Restaurant managers struggle to update their online menus efficiently, leading to delays.”
> 

Ask:

- Who experiences this?
- What do we observe happening (or not happening)?
- What evidence do we already have?

### Step 2: Generate Hypotheses for Why

Explore a range of **possible causes**. These should be *explanations*, not solutions.

For each hypothesis:

- List **supporting or contradicting evidence** (quant, qual, or anecdotal).
- Rate your **confidence level** (low / medium / high).
- Note **gaps** that require discovery work.

Example:

| Hypothesis | Evidence | Confidence | Gaps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current tools are unintuitive | 70% of managers failed usability tasks | High | Need task completion data |
| Managers prefer delegating | Engineers report frequent requests | Medium | No direct interview evidence |

### Step 3: Identify the Core Problem

Cluster related hypotheses into themes and align as a group on **the primary problem to solve**.

Use this statement structure:

> “[Customer] needs to [achieve goal] but struggles because [validated problem].
> 
> 
> This prevents [desired outcome].”
> 

Example:

> “Restaurant managers need to update menus quickly but struggle because existing tools are unintuitive and require engineering support, slowing their operations and impacting customer satisfaction.”
> 

### Step 4: Articulate the Problem Hypothesis

Document your **problem hypothesis** in full:

1. **Observation:** The visible pain or unmet need.
2. **Underlying causes:** What’s driving it (your tested hypotheses).
3. **Evidence and confidence:** Data points supporting or refuting it.
4. **Discovery plan:** What you’ll test next to strengthen confidence.

### Step 5: Draft the Solution Hypothesis (optional at this stage)

Only after the problem hypothesis feels validated, outline **how you might solve it**, ensuring traceability to the original problem.

Structure:

1. **Validated problem:** Reference the statement above.
2. **Proposed solution:** One-line summary.
3. **Assumptions:** Behavioural, usability, and impact beliefs that must be tested.
4. **Evidence:** Early signals or discovery data.
5. **Metrics:** Clear success measures tied to company or customer outcomes.

## 🧠 What “Good” Looks Like

| Weak Problem Statement | Strong Problem Hypothesis |
| --- | --- |
| “We need a new reporting dashboard.” | “Merchants can’t understand their daily performance because existing insights are fragmented across tools, causing missed sales opportunities.” |
| Focuses on a **solution** | Focuses on the **customer’s struggle** |
| Based on assumptions | Based on evidence & testable hypotheses |
| No clear outcome | Explicit measure of success |

## ✅ Output

By the end of this session, you should have:

1. A **validated problem hypothesis** with supporting evidence and confidence levels.
2. A **clear discovery plan** for remaining unknowns.
3. A **succinct statement** that anchors downstream ideation, validation, and delivery.

Use this output to populate the “Context & Problem Definition” and “Problem Validation” stages in your discovery tracker.

## 🧩 Example Output Summary

| Element | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Observation** | Restaurant managers struggle to update menus quickly. |
| **Why (Hypotheses)** | Tools are unintuitive; dependency on engineers; low perceived urgency. |
| **Evidence Summary** | 70% failed usability tasks; 80% report urgency; validated through interviews. |
| **Confidence Level** | High on usability, low on delegation. |
| **Discovery Plan** | Usability testing & survey on prioritisation. |
| **Validated Problem Statement** | Managers can’t update menus due to complex tools, causing delays and lost revenue. |
| **Solution Hypothesis (optional)** | Introduce a no-code menu editor to reduce update time from 3 days to 24 hours. |
| **Success Metric** | -50% engineer tickets, +15% revenue opportunity conversion. |
