# Discovery brief: "Studios want no-show fees"

_Skill: discovery · Fictional example_

## The signal, as it arrived
Founder: "Studios keep asking for no-show fees. Three asked this month.
Let's build them."

## Restated as a question
Why are studios asking for fees, and what job would fees do for them?

## What we looked at
- 40 support tickets mentioning cancellations or no-shows (last 90 days)
- 6 calls with studio owners (4 owner-instructors, 2 multi-instructor)
- Booking data: cancellations by how far ahead of class they happen

## What we found
1. **True no-shows are rare.** Around 3% of bookings. Late
   cancellations (less than 12 hours before class) are about 11%.
2. **The pain is the empty spot, not the customer.** Every owner
   described the lost revenue. None described wanting to punish anyone.
3. **Owners won't charge regulars.** Four out of six said they'd switch
   fees off, or waive them, for long-standing customers. That's most of
   the people who cancel.
4. **Waitlists exist but don't work.** Popular classes have waitlists,
   but spots freed up late rarely get filled because nobody's checking.

## Opportunity (reframed)
Help studios re-fill late-cancelled spots automatically, so a
cancellation doesn't cost them the revenue.

## Riskiest assumptions
| Assumption | Importance | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Waitlisted customers will accept a spot at 2 to 12 hours' notice | High | None |
| Owners will trust the app to fill spots without checking | Medium | Some (two said "yes please") |
| More notifications won't annoy customers into opting out | Medium | None |

## Recommendation
Don't build fees. Size the re-fill opportunity, then test the top
assumption with a small pilot before writing a full PRD.

## Hands off to
Market sizing (rough: what's a late-cancelled spot worth across our
studios?), then a PRD scoped to a pilot.
