# Skill: Stakeholder Update

## Purpose
Write updates people actually read. Most status updates are an activity
log ("we did a lot of things"). A good one tells the reader what changed,
what it means, and what you need from them, in that order, in under a
minute of reading.

## When to use
- Weekly or fortnightly update to founders or leadership
- Monthly product section of an investor update
- A launch announcement to the wider company
- Escalating a risk or a blocked decision

## Inputs needed
- The audience and what they care about
- What happened since the last update (rough notes are fine)
- Anything you need from them: a decision, a resource, an intro

## Context first (pm-os)
Read `context/bets.md` and `context/metrics.md`. The update should report
against the bets and metrics people already agreed to, so readers can see
progress, not just activity.

## Step-by-step process

**1. Pick the one thing.**
If the reader remembers one line, what should it be? Put it first.

**2. Write it in this order.**
1. **Headline:** the one thing, in a sentence
2. **Metrics:** the two or three numbers that matter, with direction
   and a comparison (last week, target)
3. **Bets:** each current bet as On track / At risk / Off track, one line
   each, with why for anything not on track
4. **Decisions or help needed:** specific, with a date ("I need a yes/no
   on X by Thursday")
5. **What we learned:** one or two things, especially the surprising ones
6. **Next:** what happens before the next update

**3. Cut it in half.**
Then check that nothing in the "help needed" section got cut.

**4. Match the audience.**
- Founders: decisions and risks first
- Investors: metrics and learning first, no internal jargon
- Wider team: the why and the customer impact
- Escalation: the problem, the options, your recommendation, the
  deadline. Nothing else.

**5. Humaniser pass.**

## Output
A ready-to-send update. Template: `templates/stakeholder-update-template.md`.

## Rules
- Bad news goes in the update it happened in, not the one after.
- Never report a metric without a comparison.
- "Help needed" must be specific enough that someone can say yes or no.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] First line makes sense on its own
- [ ] Every metric has a comparison
- [ ] Every at-risk bet says why
- [ ] Asks are specific and dated
- [ ] Readable in under a minute

## Hands off to
- **Decision Log**: when a decision comes back
- **Prioritisation and Roadmap**: if the update exposes that a bet should
  move
